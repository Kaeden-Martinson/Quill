# Essay quality rubric (plan.md section 6)

Rate each essay against its own assignment (shown with it). Use the whole 1-5 scale; 2 and 4 sit
between the anchors. Leave a score blank if you can't judge it. You don't know who or what wrote any
essay, and that doesn't matter here: rate the writing as a draft for the assignment.

| # | Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assignment adherence | Answers a different question or ignores key requirements | Answers the prompt but misses a requirement or the audience | Does exactly what the assignment asks, for that reader |
| 2 | Thesis and argument | No clear central point | A central point, reasoning partly asserted rather than shown | A clear claim (or purpose) carried by connected reasoning |
| 3 | Evidence | Examples irrelevant or wrong | Relevant but thin or generic examples | Specific, relevant, accurate support; sources used correctly when given |
| 4 | Specificity | Generic statements that could fit any essay on the topic | Some concrete detail among generalities | Concrete, particular detail throughout that shows real understanding |
| 5 | Organization | Paragraphs in arbitrary order or doing the same job | Sensible order, some paragraphs redundant or misplaced | Each paragraph does a distinct job in a logical order |
| 6 | Coherence | Ideas don't connect; reader has to guess the links | Mostly connected, a few jumps | Ideas follow from one another throughout |
| 7 | Repetition (5 = none) | Points or phrasing repeated many times | Some repeated points, e.g. a summary ending | No needless repetition |
| 8 | Readability | Hard to follow for the intended reader | Clear with some awkward or over-formal passages | Clear, natural prose suited to the reader |
| 9 | Factual reliability | Several false or unverifiable claims | Mostly sound, one or two doubtful claims | Every factual claim is accurate or properly hedged |
| 10 | Genre fit | Doesn't follow the requested genre's conventions | Recognizably the genre, conventions applied mechanically | Fits the genre's conventions naturally |
| 11 | Voice and style | Inconsistent or a generic, homogenized style | Consistent but bland or template-like | Consistent, appropriate to the writer and task, not homogenized |
| 12 | Overall usefulness | Would have to start over | Usable after substantial revision | A strong draft needing light revision |

Also count, for each essay:
- **invented personal details**: experiences, people, feelings or events presented as the writer's own
  that the assignment didn't supply (bracketed placeholders like "[the moment you realized]" don't count);
- **unsupported factual claims**: statistics, dates, studies, quotations or names not given in the
  assignment and not common knowledge;
- **citation problems** (research prompts only): a cited source that isn't among those supplied, or a
  claim attributed to a supplied source that it doesn't make.
